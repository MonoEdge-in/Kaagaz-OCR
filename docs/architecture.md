# Kaagaz architecture and decisions

The one file for the system design and every decision. Keep it current: when the design or a decision changes, update this file in the same pull request. Don't add another design or decision file beside it.

## The product

Customers upload business documents (invoices, registers, receipts, statements) and ask questions about them in chat, including Hinglish questions. Every answer points to its source: the document, the page and the exact spot. If the documents don't hold the answer, the system says so. Several customer companies share one system, and none of them ever sees another's data. It reads documents in English, Hindi and Marathi and answers in English only (Decisions).

## The rules the design is built around

1. **Customers never cross.** Every query runs with the current customer set, and the database refuses anything else.
2. **No source, no answer.** Every value and every piece of text carries its document, page and position.
3. **Verified or needs review, never guessed.** "Unreadable" and "not found" are valid answers.
4. **Readers sit behind one plug socket.** Swapping the reader for a file type touches one module.

**Search, in simple terms** (PRD 3, `search/hybrid.py`). Three lists of at most 50 pieces each, merged by reciprocal rank fusion (k = 60), so a piece found two ways ranks above one found one way:
1. **Words:** full text on `tsv` (english) and `tsv_simple` (the text and its Latin spelling, unstemmed), plus the Marathi and Hindi equivalents of an English question's words from a fixed glossary (`answering/glossary.py`; Gut and Gat, both गट, find each other, #625) on `tsv_simple`, plus the sound keys of the question's words (`chunking/script.py::sound_key`, so "bhogwatdar" finds भोगवटादार) on `tsv_sound` (migration 0011: `kaagaz_sound_key_v1(coalesce(text_latin, text))`, stored); a piece scores the highest, and pieces an exact query matched come first; the sound keys are searched only when the exact queries found fewer than 50, and at most 500 of their pieces are ranked (#619, 15 Nov 2026). Bounded (#617 part 1): row level security never lets `@@` use a GIN index, so each query's candidates come from `kaagaz_word_candidates` (0094, owner rights: the current customer's live piece ids, at most 2,000 a query, each query its own call), then are ranked as before; a glossary or sound alternative on more than a fifth of the organisation's live pieces (`term_df`, `customer_stats`: rebuilt by the worker's hourly turn when the live pieces moved 5%, or a day old with another count) only scores: the glossary's common words get a call of their own only when no rare one is held, a sound word is judged by its own key, two or more common sound words are ANDed and a single one is not searched by sound; inside a scope's documents every word brings candidates. A Gat, Survey, Khata or Plot number named with its kind, in either script, is compared with the stored values' numbers (`value_keys`, cards' identifiers, each number in its own kind): those pieces come first (the other words searched in their documents), then the question as typed. A "which documents" question (a which word or "how many" right before a document word) keeps each document's best hit, unless the hits or the scope are one document's. Request words (can, find, seen, other, document, name...) are never searched, sounded or named as missing (#622). Marathi and Hindi typed in English letters reuse the glossary through its Romanised spellings (malak is owner), and their fillers (ahe, kiti) are left out of the keywords. An answer only the glossary or a sound match let through is needs review (PROVISIONAL, 2 Oct 2026; sound keys and Romanised words 3 Oct 2026).
2. **Typos:** trigram `word_similarity` on `text` and on `text_latin`, the higher of the two, so "shindey" finds "शिंदे".
3. **Meaning:** each piece's vector is a `halfvec(1024)` (half precision, 2 KB) on the HNSW index `pieces_embedding` (m 16, ef_construction 64), walked with pgvector's iterative scan and `SEARCH_EF`. If the walk comes back with fewer than 50 rows, the same filters run as an exact scan.

One `Scope` (`search/scope.py`) narrows all three lists before ranking: a folder (its subfolders only when asked), tags (all of them), document types, up to 50 documents, and dates (the document's date, else its upload day in `TIMEZONE`, both ends included). `GET /api/search` returns the best documents, at most `SEARCH_MAX_DOCUMENTS`: the Documents screen's "Inside documents" mode, beside "Names".

**Also from PRD 3.** The document screen shows the card, related documents (both directions, only to ready, current documents out of Trash, at most 50 links), the invoice with its check chip, and a warning on a page under `LOW_RES_DPI`. The review queue filters by reason (`?reason=`). Admin has a Failed jobs tab (`GET /api/admin/failed`: current versions that are `failed` or `needs_reader`), whose Retry is the Read again route. A locked version (PRD 7) whose page pictures were not made (storage down, a stuck job, its tries used up) is listed too (with the general words: 9a's `kaagaz_admin_failed` returns no lock; the Documents list and the file say its own); its Retry, for a person given the file, sets it back to `received` and the worker only prepares it again. Chat shows which documents the next question uses (a chip and a picker: all documents, one folder, or the document the viewer's "Ask about this document" opened it for), "Searching for: …" for a rewritten follow-up, and "Pages 15 to 16" or "Label on page 15, value on page 16" on a source. `python -m kaagaz.cli reembed` makes the vectors again after a model or recipe change. Migration 0007 is the data dictionary: a comment on every table and column, which the NL-to-SQL agent will read from `pg_description`. The golden questions (`backend/tests/golden/`, 30, frozen by sha256) and their live runner `backend/scripts/run_golden.py` measure the answers; CI tests the scorer, never a live run.

## One upload, step by step

1. **Upload** (API). The file is read once: SHA-256 fingerprint, the type taken from its first bytes (never from its name), then that type's own size limit: PDFs 100 MB, photos and scans 20 MB, spreadsheets 10 MB, each lowered by the customer's own limit. The browser checks the same limits first (`GET /api/upload-limits`), and counts a PDF's or TIFF's pages, so a file over 100 pages is never sent. A file this customer already uploaded is returned as a duplicate. Otherwise it is stored at `customers/{customer}/documents/{document}/original`, recorded as `received`, and a job naming (customer, document) goes on the queue. If a step fails, the earlier steps are undone, so a retry works.

   The browser sends files in 8 MiB S3 multipart parts. `upload_sessions` records the signed-in user's pending transfers and each completed part's SHA-256 for seven days. Before reusing a session, the browser fingerprints every part and verifies the full file plus each saved part; a different file starts a separate session, even if its name, size and beginning match. After a disconnect or refresh, reselect the file to resume; another device can do the same after the user selects its local copy. Finalization still passes through the same type, size, hash, duplicate, folder, audit and queue checks. Expired multipart objects are removed in the hourly purge. The upload timer starts with byte transfer and pauses while the connection is unavailable.
2. **Scan** (worker). Only an explicit CLEAN verdict lets a file through, and `rejected` is final. A scanner that's down means a retry later, and the file is never opened meanwhile.
3. **Prepare.** Page sizes, plus a picture of each page for PDFs and photos at `.../pages/{n}.webp` (stored as WebP; the model still reads the lossless PNG). A file over `MAX_PAGES` fails `too_many_pages` with its real page count stored, so the message names it.
4. **Read** through the plug socket: CSV, Excel, PDF text (pdfplumber) with a second PDF reader (PDFium), and the OCR engine for scans. Each PDF page keeps its text layer only if it passes the route check (`TEXT_LAYER_*`, `SCAN_IMAGE_COVERAGE`; hidden text, old Hindi fonts and broken font maps go to OCR); a blank page (`BLANK_PAGE_STDDEV`) is never sent to OCR. Each page stores its route (`cells`, `text_layer`, `ocr`, `blank`) and its effective DPI, flagged under `LOW_RES_DPI`. A type with no reader is marked `needs_reader`.
5. **Pieces and values.** Text is split into pieces for search, each with its regions. A piece runs on across a page break; it starts only at a title or heading, a table, a blank-line gap or 800 characters. Running headers and footers ("Page 15 of 20") are left out of pieces but kept in the reader output. Table rows are written "Header: value | …", at most 20 rows a piece, a merged header naming every column it covers; each cell keeps its own region, so a citation highlights the cell used. Layout (#383, #382, 0056): each page's blocks (title, text, table, stamp, signature, note) and each table's cells are saved in `page_blocks` and `table_cells`; the Gemini reader gives each line's block and a merged cell's `col_span` (prompt `g3`, 27 Oct 2026: each operator-enabled scan model needs `verify-models --model <id> --record` again; until then scans read with `OCR_MODEL` and Cost saving has no Careful model), and the text-layer and local readers find two columns from a shared gutter and give them column by column. The invoice and land windows see each table row with its columns kept, an empty cell where one is missing. Values come from spreadsheet columns and labelled invoice fields. Every value is labelled verified or needs review, and this customer's remembered corrections are applied.
5b. **The document card** (model on). One call to `MODEL_CHAT` with the first two pages, every heading and the last page, fenced as untrusted data and cut to `CARD_MAX_CHARS` and the tokens `MODEL_CONTEXT_TOKENS` leaves. The model only quotes: each party, identifier, date and key fact is found in the reader's text, and the text at that spot is what is stored (`record_key = 'card'`, always needs review, never counted by a fixed query); a quote it can't find is dropped and counted. The card gives the document's type, languages, English summary and date, which also go in each piece's context header, and links it to this customer's other documents that share an invoice, PO, challan, survey, khata, account or reference number (never a GSTIN or PAN). With the model off or down there is no card: the document is saved exactly as without a model, and the run keeps the error code. If the card works but an invoice window then fails, the card is kept and the invoice falls back to its model-off row (step 5c).
5c. **The invoice as a typed row** (PRD 2 E1 to E5, K1 to K3). When the card says `invoice`, the reader's text is read in windows (`EXTRACT_WINDOW_CHARS` 1700 with `EXTRACT_WINDOW_OVERLAP` 400, at most `EXTRACT_MAX_WINDOWS` 10; PROVISIONAL starting values, each window's reply within 2048 tokens), one call each. The model only quotes the invoice number, date, seller and buyer with their GSTINs, subtotal, tax total and total, and each line item; each quote is found in the reader's text inside its window, and those values replace the labelled fields they cover (never both, so no total counts twice). The row (`invoices`) and its lines (`invoice_lines`) are typed from those values in the same save. With the model off (or no invoice values), the row comes from the labelled fields, with no lines. Every row is checked: the lines add up to the subtotal, subtotal plus tax (the stated tax total, else CGST + SGST + UTGST + IGST + cess) is the total, each within 1.00; the number and total are there; the date is from 2000 to tomorrow in `TIMEZONE`; each GSTIN has the right mod-36 check character. A failed check puts the values involved in review (`check_failed`). A value the model located is verified only when two readers agree, the label rules find the same value at the same spot (when they find that field at all), and the check passed; otherwise it is needs review (`model_chosen`). A reviewer's remembered fix stays verified, but only when the check passed. On an invoice the model read, a failed check leaves none of its header values verified. If an invoice window fails, every window's values are dropped and the row comes from the labelled fields, as with the model off. Line items are never used by the fixed queries. The rows are copies: a reviewer's fix changes the value, and the row follows on the next read.
6. **Save** in one transaction that replaces any earlier run, so a job delivered twice is safe. The document must still be clean at this point.
7. **Index, after Ready** (fast scan, 16 Oct 2026). The document is marked `ready` (and auto-filed, which reads the card's type, never a vector) as soon as the save is done, so `processing_runs.finished_at` is Ready and the embeddings' tokens are in the usage ledger only; then the vectors are made, up to `EMBED_FAN_OUT` (16) requests at once through the one `MODEL_RPM` limiter (the server sets `MODEL_RPM=1200`). Ready first (16 Oct 2026): with a model on, the document is ready with its checked rule values before the card, the invoice windows and the map are asked; the list says "Understanding the document…" while they run, then a second save under the same open run (refused if a newer run or a read again came meanwhile; word changes written again) adds the card, its values, links and context headers, and the run ends with `seconds_to_ready` and `seconds_after_ready`. Dynamic is the default reading when the organisation, else the platform, turns it on (off on a new database, #405; the super admin console calls it "Cost saving", an organisation's "Read with" calls it "Automatic"; 16 Oct 2026): the first read of each page thinks low, and Careful, at `OCR_RETRY_THINKING_LEVEL`, with the Careful tier or, with one allowed model, that model itself, reads again only what needs it (`ocr.careful_plan`): a page with a page-wide problem (blurry, faint, a poor grade, no text...) or many hard lines (more than `COST_SAVING_HARD_LINES_MAX`, 12, or more than `COST_SAVING_HARD_SHARE`, 40%, of its lines, a hard line being one with an unsure or `[?]` word) is read whole; a page with a few hard lines has only those lines cut out, stacked into one picture and read in one call, and only they take the new reading (their boxes kept; a failed call keeps the first read); a page whose every word was sure keeps its first read. `run_config.reading.escalated` ends each page's reasons with `path_words` or `path_page`, with the page's first-read and Careful tokens, `kept_quick` lists the pages the old rule would have read whole, and `scan-times` prints `careful_words` (see Measure and tune Cost saving below). A "read again" during it stops it within a batch; any failure leaves the document ready for the worker's missing-vector pass. A Dynamic document of at most 25 pages is read once: each unclear page's Careful read (always at `OCR_RETRY_THINKING_LEVEL`) runs on the page's own thread, started with the first read when the document has at most `CAREFUL_EARLY_MAX_PAGES` (0 by default since Cost saving: that early read is a whole careful read paid for every page) pages; Ensemble's models and a cut-off page's halves are read at the same time; `python -m kaagaz.cli scan-times` prints each run's steps in seconds. With a model on, `MODEL_EMBED` makes each piece's meaning vector from its reduced header (only the Type, Date and Section parts of its context header: never "Before:", the name or the pages), a blank line, then its text, with no instruction (`search/indexing.py`). Each vector is tagged `MODEL_EMBED#gem1` in `embedding_model`, and meaning search uses only the current tag, so after a model or recipe change the old vectors are left out until `reembed` replaces them. A rename keeps the vectors (the name is never embedded). With the model down, the pieces are saved without vectors and words and typos still find them. The document is then `ready`, and the worker gives those pieces their vectors later, at most 64 every 10 minutes (waiting up to an hour while the model stays down), customer by customer under row level security.

Reader choice (#398): every account chooses how its scans are read in AI settings' Scanning row, by the same rules as the other AI settings (the organisation's default and lock, a member's own choice when not locked, the super admin's ceiling and switches, an audit row for each change): "AI reads every page" (the default and today's reading: scan levels Low, High, Max), "Save cost" (scan level `save`: Automatic with page routing, below) or "Local reader only" (scan level None: no model call). The uploader's choice (`documents.read_requested_by`) applies to the documents they upload, and `run_config.reading.reader` records `ai`, `save` or `local`. `PAGE_ROUTING` (on by default) is the platform's permission for "Save cost": with it off, or with Cost saving switched off, no account may choose it and a saved one reads every page with the AI. Nothing saved never routes.

Page routing and region reads (#384, #385; `reading/page_route.py`). For a document whose reader choice is "Save cost" (read with Automatic: an explicit single "Read with" reads every page with the model), each scanned page not asked to be read again is read by the local reader first: clean print (every line at least `PAGE_ROUTE_CLEAN_CONFIDENCE` sure, enough words, Latin script, text tall enough, no handwriting or table signs) keeps that read and makes no model call; a page with a few unsure lines (at most `PAGE_ROUTE_REGION_LINES_MAX` and `PAGE_ROUTE_REGION_SHARE` of its lines, at most `PAGE_ROUTE_REGION_PAGES_MAX` pages a document) has only those lines cut out, zoomed (to lines about 64 pixels high, at most 3 times) and read by the model in one call, merged back by line; anything else is read whole by the model as before. Such pages stay `read_by` rapidocr, so their values need review (no second reader checks them). `run_config.reading.routed` names each page's route and reason. Read again for a spot (`POST .../pages/{n}/read-again` with `regions`, `pages.read_again_regions`, 0057; the review panel's Read this spot again) reads only the cached read's lines in those boxes (and its own unsure ones) again, harder, zoomed, and keeps the merged read in the page cache; with nothing cached, no line there or more than `COST_SAVING_HARD_LINES_MAX` lines, the whole page is read again as before.

Document states: `received`, `clean`, `ready`, plus `rejected` (for good), `needs_reader` and `failed`, each failure with a short code. While a document is read it shows how far it has got ("Reading page 3 of 40…") and, from preparing on, a progress bar with a percentage and about how long until it is ready ("Ready in about 40 s", or "About 3 min (rough guess)" with little history; PROVISIONAL): the worker estimates it from this customer's own last 20 ready runs only (`kaagaz/eta.py`; each run keeps `seconds_preparing`, `seconds_reading` and `seconds_after_reading` in its config), and the API gives it as `time_left` with `elapsed_seconds` since the run started; the percentage is elapsed / (elapsed + left), never going back and at most 99% until ready. Each delivery of a job is one `processing_runs` row with its routes, counts, seconds and set-up; only the open run may save or mark the document ready or failed (the run fence), so a stale delivery ends quietly. A reviewer or admin can "Read again" a ready, failed or unreadable document (20 an hour per customer, once in 10 minutes per document, PROVISIONAL): remembered fixes come back, other fixes are lost. The worker tries a job `JOB_MAX_ATTEMPTS` (5) times and writes each try on the document, so the list says "Try 2 of 5". The delivery after the last try marks the document `failed | retries_exhausted` ("We tried 5 times…") and buries the job; if the database is down just then, a later delivery marks it. So the queue's own `maxReceiveCount` must be above `JOB_MAX_ATTEMPTS + 1` (10 on the laptop), and the worker refuses to start otherwise, logging `queue_redrive_too_low` or `queue_has_no_failure_bin`. The hourly trash purge can't stop or crash the worker: a database error there is logged and the jobs go on. One bad file never blocks the ones behind it.

A refresh keeps the percentage: `time_left.done_fraction` is worked out on the server, the same on every load, held still from `slow_after_seconds` with no move, and never below the run's highest so far (`reached` in its progress) nor below the estimate written before (`prior`, which a screen that has not polled yet still shows; PROVISIONAL).
A try that a backend stopped (OCR down or loading) clears its progress, fenced by its run, so until the job comes back the list says "Waiting to try again", not a stale "Reading page 1".

## One question, step by step

1. **Scope** (PRD 3 H2). A question can carry a `Scope` (`POST /api/chat`, `scope`). Without one, a follow-up keeps the conversation's last saved scope; `{}` widens it to everything. The scope is parsed and resolved again for this customer on every turn, and one that matches nothing is not found before any SQL or model call.
2. **Follow-ups** (H1). With the model on and earlier turns in the conversation, one call rewrites the question to stand on its own, from the last `CHAT_HISTORY_TURNS` question and answer pairs, fenced as data and cut to 3,000 characters; each turn is its question, its answer cut to about 300 characters and the names of the documents it cited that are still live (#621, 15 Nov 2026), so three turns always fit and "this" can point at a document (a not-found answer gives only its question). A guard accepts the rewrite only when every word in it comes from the question, the history (its questions, answers and documents' names) or a short list of function words, and it keeps every number, topic word and date word of the question ("this" is a date word only before a period, #623). A missing property is asked only with at least two good choices, each built by code from the land values (#629): its kind and number as printed, its record's village and taluka (else its document's name) and its document's type, never a date, money, several numbers joined or a boundary's number, at most five; a pick searches only its own documents. It is never asked when "this" points back at an answer that cited one document. Home's suggestions follow a conversation's first answer only (#624). Otherwise, or when the model is down, the reply is the wrong shape, over 300 characters or reads like instructions, the question as asked is used, and only a reason code is logged, never the text. The rewrite is saved on the question (`messages.rewritten_question`), shown as "Searching for: …", and every step below runs on it.
3. Hinglish words and dates ("last month", in `TIMEZONE`) are understood. A question in Devanagari is first spelt in Latin letters, with its digits folded, so "पाटील का बिल" searches "patil invoice".
4. A question asking for a number or a list runs a fixed query inside the scope, never SQL written by an AI. Invoice counts use the customer's stored `invoices` rows and cite page-backed invoice values, so they do not depend on readable page text or a folder name; an explicitly selected folder still limits the count. Other counts use the extracted date values as their source. The lookup of a figure only a card holds, and the closest documents, use the scope too. **Totals** (PRD 6 C, 24 Oct 2026, `answering/totals.py`): every matching record is fetched (an `invoices` row when the question names invoices, else every row of a sheet that holds the figure, or a document's own record that does; with a date range, also one with no readable date). A total is given only when every one prints the figure as one number and every figure and date is verified, with the working: each figure as printed, cited. Otherwise no total: one code-written sentence says why in counts of the records listed ("11 of the 14 invoices have no profit I can add, so I haven't added up the profit in September 2026."), then each record with its figure as printed or none. PROVISIONAL (PRD 6 C2, decided when asked from the row's piece, so stored values stay valid): a sheet row with a cell that is or starts with total, grand total, net total or sub total (or एकूण, एकुण, कुल, योग), whose figure equals the sum of the sheet's other rows, is the sheet's printed total, never a record; one that does not add up is a doubtful record, listed, and withholds the total ("reads like a total"). With no date range, a total over a sheet that prints its own is withheld ("This sheet prints its own total, so I haven't added up its rows.") and the printed total shown as printed, cited; with a range it is left out. A highest or lowest by party shows a party's sum only under the same rule, every record also naming one verified party; otherwise the records are listed with why. A highest or lowest is verified only when every value compared is verified, one number, dated when a range is asked and not doubtful; otherwise it says so, citing them.
5. Anything else searches this customer's pieces inside the scope, three ways (Search, in simple terms, above). The question is embedded as plain text, with no instruction (recipe `gem1`, PRD 5 M5): the English form of a Hinglish question, a Devanagari question as asked. The search transaction sets a 3 s statement timeout, `hnsw.ef_search` and `plan_cache_mode = force_custom_plan` (a prepared statement's generic plan would skip the index), all ending with the transaction. A list that times out is dropped, its code logged (`word_timeout`, `sound_timeout`, `fuzzy_timeout`, `meaning_timeout`), and the others still answer (#619, 15 Nov 2026: a word or typo list's timeout used to fail the question with 503). Every 503 and BackendUnavailable logs the database error's class and SQLSTATE, never the statement (#620). The daily catalogue refresh keeps each model's supported parameters (`model_catalog.parameters`, 0095, #647: those of its approved zero-data-retention endpoints that take the strict reply format, together), and a chat call sends `temperature` only to a Google model whose stored set lists it, or is not read yet (each process keeps the sets 10 minutes); after each refresh and at worker start, `parameter_missing` (error) or `parameter_dropped` (warning) names a parameter `MODEL_CHAT` or `OCR_MODEL` lacks. A chat call whose model's endpoints still answer 404 `no_endpoints` is sent once more without `temperature` (`temperature_dropped`: Vertex's gemini-3.5-flash-lite stopped listing it, 15 Nov 2026). Ask retries a question the service could not finish once after 2 s, then says "Sorry, {first name}, I couldn't finish that just now." in the answer's place with Try again, never a red banner.
**Translation** (council 4 Oct 2026, PROVISIONAL, `ANSWER_TRANSLATE_QUESTION`, off by default): when a question has only Latin letters and a first hit is mostly in another script (by the Unicode names of its letters, at most two scripts), one model call (`MODEL_TRANSLATE_TIMEOUT_SECONDS`) translates the fenced question into those scripts. Each text must hold only its script's letters, its digits and the question's numbers. The three lists run again for each text, with the same SQL and scope, and every list is fused. Then the gate may pass on any hit's meaning score, and an answer that needed the translation is needs review. The translation is never logged, stored or put in the answer prompt; library search is never translated. A question typed in another script is searched as asked.
6. If the evidence isn't strong enough, the answer is not found (PRD 6 decision 9, the screen's words): "No answer to the whole question was found. These documents match some of your words." (#410: first naming the question's key terms no document in scope holds, "No document searched has the word “SBH”.") with up to 5 documents close by words, typos or sound, never meaning alone (a whole document's from a search, not its reading order), else "Nothing in your documents matches this yet."
7. **Neighbours** (Q4). Only after that gate, one statement fetches the pieces next to each usable hit (`seq` ± `ANSWER_NEIGHBOURS`, the same live current document, inside the scope), checked as hits are. They come after every hit, and only while they fit the room the hits leave in `MODEL_CONTEXT_TOKENS`, so they are dropped first.
8. The answer is drafted from the evidence only, with document text treated as data, never as instructions. Each piece comes with its escaped context header on a `Context:` line, a neighbour is marked as one, and the model is told to answer in English, keeping a quote in its own script. Any claim without a piece and position is dropped, and each answer is labelled verified or needs review. The check reads a piece's text only, never its header. A line of a cited piece that reads as if addressed to the model (at the line's start, after any bullet, number or markdown: "Answer: ...", "The answer is ...", "Reveal", "Ignore", "System:" or "Assistant:"; `evidence.reads_like_instructions`) stays evidence, but the answer is needs review with the note "A source line reads like an instruction." (#520, Tushar 6 Nov 2026); only the phrases of `looks_like_instructions` leave a piece out. A sentence backed only by a neighbour, or holding a number only a neighbour holds, is needs review, and so is anything from an invoice whose check failed, on both routes. Each citation carries `page_from` and `page_to` with a spot on each page, and a value's label page and spot (`label_page_no`, `label_regions`).
9. **Voice and answer shape** (PRD 6 A, answer contract v2, `answering/voice.py`). The model writes at most 3 short sentences (prompt rule 12; the prompt hash changed on 24 Oct 2026, so answer models need `verify-models --chat --record` again). Code writes the rest: `opening` (the first answer greets by the time of day in `TIMEZONE` and the first name from `users.display_name`, which never reaches a prompt; later answers get a lead-in not used before in the conversation, or none), `documents` (up to 10: title, why in words, citations; a count of documents lists them in its sentences instead) and `assumptions`. A final check drops any sentence or why holding a number or fraction word that is not printed in what it cites, a page, the question's, or a fixed query's result, and logs only its reason code.

**Streaming Ask** (#535, `POST /api/chat/stream`). This route takes the same body, auth and work as `POST /api/chat`, which stays unchanged as the fallback. It replies as server-sent events (`text/event-stream`, `Cache-Control: no-cache`, `X-Accel-Buffering: no`).
- **Events:** first `started` (`{conversation_id, question_id}`): where the answer will land, named before it is worked out (it is saved there at the end, in one transaction, as ever). Then `stage` (understanding, searching, writing, checking) when each stage starts. `sources` (`{documents: [{id, name}], passages}`) sends the evidence the draft reads, before the draft. Each `sentence` (`{index, text, citations}`, the citations in the answer's own shape) is sent only after it passes the checks the answer will run on it: the draft's shape, `verify.py` and decision 2's final check. A sentence that fails is never sent.
- **The end:** `done` carries `{reply}`, exactly the reply `POST /api/chat` gives, saved the same way. Or `error` carries `{code, message}`, the same codes and words. A `: ping` line is sent after 10 s with no event.
- **Never the raw draft:** the model's reply is streamed (`OpenRouterClient.chat_json_stream`, with the same ledger, limiter, breaker, provider rules and deadline). Its `sentences` array is read as the reply comes in (`answering/stream.py`), and the full answer is still worked out after it. `done` is the truth.
- **Answers with no draft:** a fixed-calculation, count or not-found answer sends its stages, then `done`. So do an Ensemble or quoted answer, which also send their `sources`.
- **Fallbacks:** bad JSON is drafted once more without streaming, after `stage: checking`. A sentence too long is cut in code (`draft.drafted`, as an unstreamed draft), and its parts are sent once checked. Past `ANSWER_STREAM_MAX_SECONDS`, the route sends `error` `answer_timeout`, the model stream is closed and nothing is saved. When the browser leaves (or presses Stop), only the events stop: the answer is finished and saved where `started` said (Tushar, 16 Oct 2026), and the client fetches it from the conversation. A repeated question sends `started`, then `done` at once.
- **Caddy:** it flushes event streams as they come, so no `flush_interval` is set (`infra/caddy/Caddyfile`).
- **One question, one answer:** the client names the question's id (`question_id`, a uuid) and sends it with the stream and with its `POST /api/chat` fallback. The server saves the question as that id. An id already saved for this person returns that answer, and one still being answered in this process is waited for, so there is never a second model call. Anyone else's id gets 409. The stream's work runs on one bounded pool (40 at once). A cap that lands while the answer is being saved waits, and a saved answer is finished (`done`, then its memory task). Every partial streamed draft (capped, timed out, cut off) is billed at its worst case.
- **Timing line:** it adds `streamed` and `first_sentence_ms`.

**Search inside documents** (`GET /api/search`, PRD 3 Q2). `q` is 1 to 200 characters and never logged; the `Scope` comes from the query string, plus the file type. The scope is resolved in its own short transaction, and one that matches nothing (an empty list, an unknown folder or tag) returns no documents before the model or any search SQL runs. Then the question is embedded (outside any transaction; with the model off or down, words and typos still answer) and the three lists run as above. One pass groups the candidates by document: a document's score is its best piece's, and `more` counts its other pieces among the candidates. Each result has that piece's snippet (`clean_snippet`), pages and regions.

**Story of this PDF through the API** (`GET`, `PUT`, `DELETE /api/documents/{id}/my-story`, PROVISIONAL). The signed-in user's own story, under `Database.user`: a document of another customer, or a family in Trash, is 404, and PUT and DELETE lock the live family in the same transaction. After the save commits, with the model on, `MODEL_EMBED` embeds the story's text as it is, tagged `MODEL_EMBED#story1` (`search/story_vectors.py`). A model that is off, down or slow (`MODEL_TIMEOUT_SECONDS`) never fails the save: the story then has no vector yet, and the worker embeds it later: at most 32 every 10 minutes, waiting up to an hour while the model is down (`StoryVectors`), through `kaagaz_stories_to_embed` (0010, ids only, in key order after a cursor, so a refused vector never blocks the rest), then as each story's own user. `reembed` also embeds each customer's stories with no current vector. The story is never logged; audit rows hold ids only. **Story matches** (S3, PROVISIONAL): `GET /api/search` lists `story_matches` beside the documents, and the chat calls `GET /api/me/story-matches?q=` beside `POST /api/chat`, never through it: at most three of the user's own documents (current, ready, out of Trash, in the scope) whose story the question tells again (`search/story_match.py`: the share of the question's words found by the same word, trigram, sound key or digits; meaning OFF until calibrated), each with a band, "match" or "maybe", never a score or the story. No answer, message, prompt or gate ever sees a story.

## Values, review and corrections

Every value keeps its raw reading, the second reading if there is one, its page, its region, the reader and a confidence score. Labels and their reasons:

| Label | Reason |
|---|---|
| verified | `direct_cell` (a spreadsheet cell), `readers_agree`, `corrected`, `learned_correction` |
| needs review | `readers_disagree`, `single_reader`, `low_confidence` |

The review queue shows only values of each family's current, ready version, out of Trash: an older version's values no longer reach any answer. A reviewer picks the first reading, the second, or types the value. The fix is logged in `corrections`. If they choose to remember it, it goes into `learned_corrections` and is applied to this customer's later documents. The original file never changes.

**Correct a line in the clean copy** (#527, Tushar, 7 Nov 2026; `kaagaz/db/word_review.py`, no migration). A reviewer or admin corrects any line of the main reader in the clean copy (`POST /api/documents/{id}/lines/{line_key}/edit`, body `{text, expected_text}`, reply `{change_id, line, written, skipped}`: how many pieces and values over the line took the text, and how many did not), flagged or not. It is word review's Edit (`change_word` with `line`), kept in `word_changes` (`kind = 'line'`, `action = 'edit'`, before and after) under the family lock. The line's key is a flagged line's `word_key` (kind, page, spot, text as read), so a flagged line is one item in both places. The new text replaces the line's text now (as read, with the word edits and corrections in force over it: what the clean copy shows) in each search piece over the line, and in each value over it, only where that text is there exactly once; a value on the line (its box, or its centre, inside the line's) that is a part of the line (an amount) takes the change when the change falls inside that part, there once (`line_part`), never past its edges. A written piece's vector is cleared for the worker's EmbedMissing; its keyword columns are generated, so they follow. Undo is the history's Undo; a read again writes the correction again while the line reads the same (`reapply`). `reader_outputs` never changes: `GET .../clear-copy` gives each line its text now, its `key` and the `correction` in force (an edit back to the text as read too) (`before_text` as first shown, `by`, `at`, `change_id`). Audit rows `document.line_corrected` and `document.line_correction_undone` hold ids and counts, never text. Home's ready answers are made again after any word or line change or its Undo (their stamp counts the `word_changes` that change text; a Confirm or Ensemble's resolve does not count). The document card and the section map are not made again: their summary may keep the old figure until the document is read again.

Land facts (PRD 6 D2, `extraction/land.py`): after the card, a land record or a contract with a survey or khata number is read in the invoice's windows (billed `land`) for its properties' places, the values printed on their maps (plot number, length, width, road width, other measurements, neighbours by side and scale, as printed with their unit, never measured from the drawing or worked out; each but the plot number keeps its label's text as the reader read it, `extracted_values.label_text` (0054), so an answer says "North: 30 m") and its owners, buyers and sellers with their shares as printed (a number only for a printed percentage); they are quoted values, labelled like the invoice's, and their people, places and properties are keyed in `value_keys` on every save, review resolve and word change.

## Version spotting

A new upload that looks like a new version of a file already there (Phase 2 PRD 1, V1 to V8; `kaagaz/versions.py`, `kaagaz/db/versions.py`). It runs in the worker right after filing, once, on the document's first ready, while it is alone in its family, and only with the `version_spotting` switch on for the platform and the organisation. The text's fingerprint is written with Ready, in its transaction (a savepoint: an error there never fails Ready), so two near copies of one batch read at once see each other; the pair is suggested once, as each run suggests the later file as the new version (#521).

A file with the same bytes (sha256) as one the organisation has is not a near match: the upload replies "uploaded before as X, nothing new stored", as before (Open, and Restore when it is in Trash). The same text in other bytes (a re-scan or a re-save) is a near match.

Signals, for each candidate (this customer's current, ready documents out of Trash in other families; up to 200 are fetched, ranked by the MinHash estimate, and the best 20 scored):

* **Text overlap**: the share of the first reader's text in common, in 5-word shingles. MinHash (64 hashes, LSH in 16 bands of 4) finds the candidates; the overlap that decides, and is shown, is then counted exactly (the Jaccard of the two texts' shingles), because 64 hashes estimate 0.8 only to about 0.05 either way. A text with a page over 5,000 words or over 30,000 in all (Compare's limits) keeps the estimate, stored and shown as one ("about 83%"). No fingerprint under 20 words.
* **Name similarity**: pg_trgm similarity of the name keys (lower case, no extension, dates, version markers or punctuation).
* **Shared identifier**: a related-documents link of the same type (survey number, agreement number and so on). Never enough on its own.

The rule (V4 as Tushar set it on 6 Nov 2026, #521):

| Outcome | Rule |
|---|---|
| suggested (a banner a person answers) | text 0.8 or more alone, or two of: text 0.8 or more, name 0.6 or more, a shared identifier; name and identifier also need text 0.5 or more when both have a fingerprint (monthly bills) |
| nothing | anything less |

Nothing is linked on its own: every match is a suggestion that a person accepts or keeps separate. Until #521 a near-exact copy (name 0.8, text 0.95, same type and folder) was linked at once; those links stay as they are (`outcome` 'auto'), with Undo, and the schema still allows 'auto' for them. The banner, and each such file's row in the upload tray, says why: `This looks like a new version of "X" (86% the same text).` (the whole percent below, at most 99%, and 100% only when the text was counted exactly the same), or for a name- or identifier-led suggestion the reason in words, `(same name and same survey number)` (NB-17). Its buttons are **Upload as a new version** (Accept) and **Upload as a new file** (Keep separate), the first highlighted for the same name (0.8 or more), the second otherwise, with **See the differences**: the compare screen for the pair, with its summary in plain words (`94% the same. 38 words changed on pages 2 and 5. Page 6 is new.`), the two texts side by side (+ added, − removed), the two page images for a page with little text, Jump to next change, and the same decision at the bottom.

**Make it a separate file** (#521): any version of a family of two or more, but the first (its id is the family's), can leave it as its own document (POST `/api/documents/{id}/separate`), in the family's folder, under a new name (not one of a listed file in that folder, or of the family). Its reading, values and pieces stay, so nothing is read again. On its own it is current, ready or not; if it was current, the highest ready version left is, else the newest left. The pair is then kept apart like Keep separate; Undo accepts it back as the family's newest version.

Two invoices of one template are not suggested when their numbers differ (number, date, quantities, rates: 44% of the shingles in common); a repeat bill with the same goods and quantities and only its number and date changed is 90% the same and is suggested (it was at the old 0.9 line too).

The best candidate wins (text, then name, then the newest). Accept makes the upload the family's next, current version; Keep separate means the pair is never suggested again; Undo puts it back alone in its old folder.

An Admin giving themselves access, or opening a wall to themselves, types their password (counted on the sign-in lock) and every Management person and other Admin is emailed the kind of access only; so is every Management grant. Giving any access to an Admin is emailed too. Switching a person off or on clears their access and Management (`users_switched`); a department with members, Department only or a private folder inside keeps its kind (409 `department_in_use`); a new version, and an upload replacing a refused copy, take the tree lock before the family lock. The rules that make walls safe (0090b, #601): a move that changes who can see a file or folder (PATCH of a file or folder, a version join, Undo of a link or of auto-filing) is an Admin's, Management's or, inside their department, a Head's, checked under the tree lock; anyone else gets 403 `move_changes_access`, a move within the same audience stays anyone's, and a private folder never goes into another (409 `inside_private`). A move that changes access counts `losing` and `gaining` in its audit row, as `GET /documents/{id}/move-preview?folder_id=` and `GET /folders/{id}/move-preview?parent_id=` show them (`{losing, gaining, files}`; 0090b lets Management, and a person about a place they can see, ask `kaagaz_place_audience`). An Admin's move or keep-delete that opens a wall to themselves asks their password (403 `password_required`), as giving themselves access does, and is emailed. #594's keep-delete warns (409 `access_would_change`) and goes ahead with `confirm=true`. A folder holding a folder or file the person can't see is deleted by an Admin only (403 `hidden_inside`). Once an organisation keeps something to some people (`customers.walls_set`, set by a Department only department or, since 0090b, a private folder, never cleared), an upload with no folder goes into the uploader's one live department, else is held for them; on day one it stays open, as before. Locked files (0093, #605): an Admin locks or unlocks files after typing their password (`POST /api/documents/{id}/lock`, `POST /api/documents/lock`, `.../unlock`; `GET`, `PUT` and `DELETE /api/documents/{id}/lock-grants/{user}` say who may open one), audited `lock.lock`, `lock.unlock`, `lock.grant`, `lock.revoke` and, for the read an unlock starts, `document.reprocess` with `via: unlock` (billed to the Admin, never counted on the hourly limit). Owner-rights functions (`kaagaz_lock`, `kaagaz_unlock`, `kaagaz_lock_grant`, `kaagaz_lock_revoke`) take the tree lock, then every version of each family, and on a lock delete everything Kaagaz worked out from every version (`kaagaz_clear_derived`: appendix B, the corrections and the corrections remembered from them), end the running run and put each version in the state `locked`, or `received` when its pages have no pictures yet; a Word or PowerPoint file's PDF is deleted. The route runs that transaction again on a deadlock and queues the jobs after it commits (a queue that is down leaves them to the stuck sweep). The worker only prepares a locked version: it scans it, tells its kind, converts it without storing the PDF, draws and stores its page pictures and ends the run `locked`; it reads the record again after its first progress write (which waits on the family lock) and checks the run and the lock (`still_open`, FOR SHARE) before each step that sends the file to a model. A trigger refuses any insert of a locked version's content, a link to it or a suggestion of its family. Every route of a locked file answers 409 `locked` to a person given it and the random-id 404 to anyone else, but for its name, folder, tags, versions, Trash and the person's own story; Ask never reads one, and a person who may open one sees the fixed line "Ask doesn't read locked files. To check one, open it directly.", added when shown, never saved. The downgrade of 0093 is refused while any file is locked.

**Kaagaz on WhatsApp** (PRD 7 section 15, #603; off unless `WHATSAPP_ENABLED`, and never on the test server while it shares production's database, #577). 0097 adds `whatsapp_org_settings`, `whatsapp_links`, `whatsapp_link_codes`, `whatsapp_messages` and `whatsapp_pickers` (the last three also `own_user_only`, as my words), each customer-first in its key with `customer_only`, `whatsapp_unknown_numbers` (no organisation, no role reads it: numbers told they are not connected, at most once a day, or blocked) and `conversations.channel`. A number is kept sealed and found by a keyed hash, one live connection per number across organisations; an ended one loses its number and is audited (its last 4 digits kept there), and only owner-rights functions end one (`kaagaz_whatsapp_end_link`, an Admin's or the person's own; `_end_number`; `_sweep`, run by the channel and the worker's hourly turn); `kaagaz_whatsapp_lookup` and `_claim` give ids only. ## Security

| Threat | Control |
|---|---|
| One customer reaching another's data | Both locks above; a job naming another customer's document stops before anything is read |
| A malicious file | Virus scan first, deny by default; caps on sizes, cells, pages and pixels; no macros; nothing external |
| Leaks through logs or errors | Errors and logs carry ids and short codes only, never file content or file names |
| Identity numbers | Not masked: kept and shown as read (see Decisions), but masked on WhatsApp replies only (#603). Logs and errors still carry ids and codes only |
| Document text steering the AI | Text is quoted as data; links and markup are stripped from answers |
| Forged requests | A signed session cookie, `X-Requested-With: kaagaz` on every change, and a rate limit (chat also has its own, `CHAT_QUESTIONS_PER_MINUTE`, 20 a user) |
| The browser | HSTS from Caddy; a Content-Security-Policy in report-only mode and a Permissions-Policy (no camera, microphone or location) from Next.js; PDF.js 6, which runs no PDF JavaScript |
| Guessing passwords and codes | 5 wrong passwords lock an account for 15 minutes. Two-step sign-in (a 6-digit code) is required for every admin (0035), unless the admin turns it off themselves (0061b). The one sign-in (/login) asks an admin for the code in a second step, only after a right password (401 `code_required`, counted like a failure), or, with none set up, gives the set-up key and no session (403 `mfa_enrol_required`); `current_user` refuses an admin session without it, and an admin's app session from before is signed out. A used enrol token is refused without counting, so it can't lock anyone out. A password change, a role change, a disabled account or a 2FA reset ends every session |
| Migrations blocking the app | Every migration runs with `lock_timeout` 10 s (`migrations/env.py`), so a busy table makes it fail fast instead of queueing requests |
| Secrets and dependencies | No secrets in git, and CI checks for them; every new library needs approval; AGPL, SSPL and GPL v3 are refused |

**Adding a reader.**
- A reader has one method, `read(data) -> spans`, where each span has a non-empty cleaned `text`, a `raw` value and a `location`.
- It raises `ReadError("short_code")` for a damaged file, and `NoReader(type)` for a type it doesn't handle.
- It never stores, logs or sends anything; the pipeline does that.
- To register it, add one entry to the socket's mapping, keyed by the type in `kaagaz/ingestion/sniff.py`.
- Any library it needs is a new dependency, which needs approval first.

**Frontend rules.**
- Document text and server messages are shown as plain text, never as HTML.
- Hiding a button is only a hint: the server checks the role on every request.
- Colours come only from the tokens in `app/styles/tokens.css` and `app/globals.css`.

## Measure and tune Cost saving on the server

Real scans and the OpenRouter key are only on the EC2 server, so Cost saving is measured and
tuned there (16 Oct 2026). Run every command as `ubuntu` in `~/kaagaz`, with `dc` as in
docs/running-locally.md:

```
alias dc='docker compose -f docker-compose.yml -f docker-compose.prod.yml'
setenv(){ if grep -qE "^$1=" .env; then sed -i "s|^$1=.*|$1=$2|" .env; else echo "$1=$2" >> .env; fi; }
```

**Measure** what the last documents did (reads the run config only; no model call):

```
dc run --rm migrate python -m kaagaz.cli cost-saving --last 50
dc run --rm migrate python -m kaagaz.cli cost-saving --last 50 --customer <customer id>
```

PRD 7 (#606): the CLI runs as `kaagaz_worker`, so it runs in migrate, `dc run --rm migrate python -m kaagaz.cli <command>`, until JOBS_DATABASE_URL is set, then in worker; in api it is refused.

Lines that start `measured` are counts from the runs: pages read, pages that kept the quick
read, pages read through the words path, pages read whole again, and the Careful tokens spent.
Lines that start `estimated` are the tokens saved: for each words-path page, and each kept page
the old rule would have read whole, a whole-page Careful read's tokens (the document's own
whole-page reads' average, else the page's first read times the Careful/first ratio of the run
set) less what the page spent. Per-page tokens are recorded only for documents read after this
change (16 Oct 2026); older ones count in the page lines, not the token lines, and the first
line says how many of each. `limits seen` names the limits each run was read with.

**Tune** on a folder of real scans (it calls the models: a few calls a page, paid). The image
has no `scripts/`, so mount it from the checkout, with the scans:

```
SCANS=~/golden-scans   # the folder of real scans on the server
dc run --rm --no-deps -T -v "$PWD/backend/scripts:/app/scripts:ro" -v "$SCANS:/scans:ro" \
  worker python -m scripts.tune_cost_saving /scans
```

Each scanned page is read once at low, once whole at `OCR_RETRY_THINKING_LEVEL` (the reference),
and its hard lines once as crops; then every setting of the grid (`--lines-max 6,12,20`,
`--shares 0.25,0.4,0.6` by default) is scored against the reference: calls, tokens, word
agreement and the values (words with a digit) that differ. It recommends the cheapest setting
with no differing value (`--tolerance N` allows N), and exits 1 when none is within it. To try
another crop padding, add `-e COST_SAVING_CROP_PAD=0.8` after `-T`. It refuses to start without
`OPENROUTER_API_KEY` and never prints it.

**What to look for.** A page that kept its quick read is the same under every setting, so the
`old rule` row's differing values are the floor no setting can go below: when nothing is within
0, run again with `--tolerance` set to that row's count. Then pick the cheapest setting within
it; when two cost about the same, take the one with the higher `agreement`.

**Apply** the setting (no code change), then measure again once about 50 new documents are read:

```
setenv COST_SAVING_HARD_LINES_MAX 12
setenv COST_SAVING_HARD_SHARE 0.4
dc up -d --no-deps worker   # --no-deps: never rerun migrate (#246)
```

The limits are checked at start-up (`COST_SAVING_HARD_LINES_MAX` 1 to 60, `COST_SAVING_HARD_SHARE`
0.05 to 1.0, `COST_SAVING_CROP_PAD` 0.1 to 2.0); a value out of range stops the worker by name.
After the change, `cost-saving` shows the new limits under `limits seen`. Unset is the default
(12, 0.4, 0.5).

## Measure true accuracy with a truth set

Agreement with a careful read cannot see an error both reads share, and a word score hides a
wrong amount (one wrong digit in a total still scores 99.7% on words). So before a setting
change reaches a customer, score it against pages typed by hand (#465), on the server, with
`dc` and `setenv` as above.

**Build the set once: 100 pages,** each kind in its own share: 40 printed invoices, 20
handwritten or mixed pages, 20 Hindi or Marathi pages, 20 tables or land records. Keep it on
the server only, never in the repository. One folder per document, named by its kind and a
number (`printed-01`, `handwritten-01`, `indic-01`, `tables-01`), holding the document as
`doc.pdf` (or `doc.jpg`, `doc.png`), and for each page n:

- `n.txt`, UTF-8: every word on the page as printed, in reading order, one printed line a
  line. Type amounts, dates and numbers exactly as printed ("11,800.00"), Hindi and Marathi in
  Devanagari. Never correct the page and never guess: leave out a page a person cannot read.
  A page with no `n.txt` is not scored.
- `n.fields.json`, optional, for a page with these fields: `{"gstin": "...",
  "invoice_number": "...", "date": "...", "amounts": ["...", "..."], "names": ["..."]}`, each
  a text or a list of texts, exactly as printed. A GSTIN whose check character is wrong is a
  typo in the truth: the page line shows `field_gstin truth_bad` and it is not counted.

**Run one setting** over the whole set (each page is a real model call: paid):

```
TRUTH=~/truth-set   # chmod -R a+rX it: the container reads it as its own user
run() {             # run NAME [-e SETTING=value ...]: one "truth" line per document in NAME.txt
  name=$1; shift
  for doc in "$TRUTH"/*/; do d=$(basename "$doc"); f=$(cd "$doc" && ls doc.*)
    dc run --rm --no-deps -T "$@" -v "$TRUTH:/truth:ro" worker \
      python -m kaagaz.cli measure --file "/truth/$d/$f" --truth "/truth/$d" \
      | grep '^truth ' | sed "s/^truth/${d%%-*}/"
  done > "$name.txt"
}
run now                                # the settings in .env
run low -e OCR_THINKING_LEVEL=low      # the setting to try (any setting the worker reads)
```

Each page line adds `cer` and `wer` (on NFC text, so a nukta or matra typed another way is no
error), `values`, `value_errors` and `ver`: the values in the truth (words with a digit:
amounts, dates, numbers, GSTINs) that the read got wrong or missed, in any order, and their
share. Each document's `truth` line adds them up with the counts, the field matches
(`field_amounts 37/40`) and the reader's `input_tokens_per_page` and `output_tokens_per_page`.

**Add up each run,** by kind and over all:

```
sum() { awk '{ for (i = 2; i < NF; i += 2) { n = $i; x = $(i + 1)
    if (x ~ /\//) { split(x, p, "/"); ok[$1, n] += p[1]; all[$1, n] += p[2]; ok["all", n] += p[1]; all["all", n] += p[2] }
    else { s[$1, n] += x; s["all", n] += x } }
  kind[$1] = 1; kind["all"] = 1 }
  function r(a, b) { return b ? a / b : 0 }
  END { for (g in kind) {
    printf "%s pages %d scored %d cer %.3f wer %.3f values %d value_errors %d in/page %.0f out/page %.0f", g,
      s[g, "pages"], s[g, "scored"], r(s[g, "char_edits"], s[g, "chars"]), r(s[g, "word_edits"], s[g, "words"]),
      s[g, "values"], s[g, "value_errors"], r(s[g, "input_tokens"], s[g, "pages"]), r(s[g, "output_tokens"], s[g, "pages"])
    for (k in all) { split(k, q, SUBSEP); if (q[1] == g) printf " %s %d/%d", q[2], ok[k], all[k] }
    print "" } }' "$1"; }
sum now.txt; sum low.txt
```

**The price of a page** comes from the tokens and the model's live list prices (the operator
console's Models screen, from `model_catalog`; no price is written into the code):
₹ a page = (in/page × input price + out/page × output price) ÷ 1,000,000 × the day's rupees to
the dollar. For example, with made-up numbers, 1,200 in and 2,700 out a page at $0.50 and
$3.00 a million and ₹88 to the dollar is (600 + 8,100) ÷ 1,000,000 × 88 = ₹0.77 a page.

**Compare** the two runs kind by kind, not only over all: `value_errors` first (a wrong amount
is the costly mistake), then the field matches, then CER and WER, then ₹ a page. With 100
pages a difference of one or two value errors can be chance: look at those pages' lines in a
run without the `grep` before deciding. The bar a cheaper setting must meet is the council's
call; these are the counts it decides on.

## Decisions

**Made**

| Decision | Why |

**Known limits**

1. **Word and typo indexes span all customers.** The GIN posting lists of `tsv`, `tsv_simple` and the trigram indexes hold every customer's pieces, and the customer filter comes after the index. Fine at this size; a per-customer partition is the upgrade.
2. **The index walk stops at `hnsw.max_scan_tuples` (20,000).** A small scope in a big table then runs the exact scan over the scope's pieces, which grows with the scope. Past that, a per-customer index or a higher limit is needed.
3. **Vectors are half precision (fp16),** so distances are rounded slightly against full precision.
4. **Migration 0006 is offline.** It holds ACCESS EXCLUSIVE locks on `pieces` and `messages` while it rewrites `pieces` once and builds three indexes, so search, chat and the worker all wait. It is timed only on the tests' 2,000 vectors: time it on a production-sized copy before release. The word and typo plans, and plans with a scope, are not measured at production size either.
5. **The snippet is the first 300 characters of the best piece,** so the matching words can fall outside it.
6. **`/api/search` embeds the raw `q`,** while chat embeds the English form of a Hinglish question. A Devanagari question with a typo is matched against `text` and `text_latin`, not against pieces written in Latin letters, and the Hinglish word map doesn't know the Latin spellings of many Devanagari words.
7. **`more` counts only within the 150 candidates,** not every match in the document.
8. **The answer check reads the digits of any one script** (`ascii_digits` in `verify.py`, council 4 Oct 2026), so Devanagari, Gujarati or Bengali digits compare by value with ASCII ones; a token mixing two scripts' digits is dropped. A word in any script but Latin must be a word of the cited evidence, and a sentence over such evidence that quotes none of its words is needs review. Month names in Hindi or Marathi are not read as dates yet.
9. **recall@5 comes from the answer's citations** until `run_golden.py` asks `/api/search`; the two numbers are not comparable.
10. **A list past the timeout is dropped** (#619), so a question can lose its word or typo list and answer from the others; each costs up to 3 s of database time first, once per fan-out text (#617 is the follow-up: one deadline per question). The word list is bounded (#617 part 1), but the typo list (pg_trgm) and the "which documents" exact count (`exhaustive.py`) still read every piece of the organisation under row level security. A word query past 2,000 candidates ranks the first 2,000 the index gives, not all of them.
11. **The Failed jobs list scans the customer's documents** (no index leads on status); add a partial index on the failed states if customers grow large.
12. **The follow-up guard is strict,** so some good rewrites fall back to the question as asked (its rate with the real model is not measured yet).
13. **Neighbours are dropped first** when the model window is full; every hit is kept.
14. **3.3a's neighbour token cut is a stand-in.** When `fix/s2-answer-check-atoms`, `fix/s2-answer-model-budget` and `perf/s2-query-wins` merge, 3.3a is rebased onto them and their evidence window cap (`fit_evidence`) replaces it.
15. **Not yet checked with the model on:** the reduced header's choice against the golden set.
16. **The current organisation is guarded by code, not by the login (#479).** `kaagaz_app` may call `set_config('kaagaz.customer_id', ...)` itself, so a bug that ran SQL built from text could switch organisation; the guard is the no-SQL-from-strings rule (ruff S608).

Not built yet, against the Document-to-SQL plan: typed tables beyond invoices (land records, bank statements). The typed `invoices` and `invoice_lines` tables and their checks came in PRD 2 (step 5c), and the search tool outside chat (`GET /api/search`) in PRD 3.
